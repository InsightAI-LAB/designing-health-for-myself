01 / The conversation

A working prototype is
only the beginning.

AI can help people create tools for their own health and wellbeing. But who decides what those tools do, what data they use, and when they should change?

We will look beyond app generation to the whole life of a personal tool: expressing a need, inspecting behavior, revising defaults, repairing problems, and choosing to stop.

This proposed workshop brings together researchers and practitioners to make those questions concrete. Through fictional scenarios, shared design work, and cross-group challenges, we will develop practical evaluation questions and a research agenda.

Our focus is personal-use, low-risk wellbeing software. We will examine boundaries around clinical functions rather than build diagnostic tools, medication advice, or crisis-response systems.

Include author names, affiliation if applicable, a contact email, and your sharing preference. No coding experience, working prototype, or personal health disclosure is required.

Who should join?

We welcome HCI, end-user development, personal informatics, digital health, accessibility, usable privacy and security, and responsible AI perspectives. Relevant lived experience is welcome without any disclosure requirement.

Two organizers will review each contribution for relevance and its potential to deepen discussion. Selection will seek a mix of disciplines, methods, career stages, and viewpoints. Accepted position-paper authors receive priority under CHI’s seating process; other CHI attendees may join within capacity.

Read the full draft call for participation +

For review only. This proposed call is not open. The route, dates, and organizer participation require approval.

How can people create health and wellbeing apps that remain understandable, changeable, and appropriate for their lives? Designing Health for Myself brings together researchers and practitioners in human–computer interaction, end-user development, personal informatics, digital health, accessibility, and responsible AI to examine AI-assisted personal app authoring.

Across two consecutive, in-person 85-minute sessions, separated by a break, we will map who controls app goals, generated behavior, data, and maintenance. Small groups will work with fictional wellbeing scenarios, challenge design assumptions, and develop practical evaluation questions. We will produce a shared research agenda, reusable authoring and control worksheets, and a synthesis of unresolved tensions. No coding experience, working prototype, or personal health disclosure is required.

We invite one- or two-page position papers, excluding references, or accessible contributions of 500–1000 words. Describe an authoring challenge, an example or design tension, and a question for discussion. Early ideas, critical perspectives, and unsuccessful prototypes are welcome. Submit to whasan@tuskegee.edu using the instructions and confirmed deadline on our website.

We expect 20–30 attendees, including organizers, and require at least ten. The workshop is open to all CHI attendees, with priority for authors of accepted position papers. At least two organizers must register and attend; the full organizing team is planned.

With permission, we will share position papers and workshop artifacts on the website as non-archival materials. Only the organizer proposal enters CHI Extended Abstracts. Publication is optional, and contributors can review attribution before release. Offline materials and an accessible summary will support later readers.

03 / The working program

Think. Make. Challenge.
Reconsider.

Two consecutive, in-person 85-minute sessions. Paper, sketches, and editable text are enough. No paid AI tool, personal device, or live internet is needed.

01

Session one · 85 minutes

From personal needs
to accountable design

  1. 00–10 MIN

    Welcome & shared boundaries

    Introductions, access options, publication choices, and fictional information only.

  2. 10–25 MIN

    Questions worth working on

    Brief facilitated rounds to surface shared tensions and conflicting perspectives.

  3. 25–55 MIN

    Fictional scenario authoring

    Explore a rest planner, reflection journal, or gentle activity-break reminder. An app is optional.

  4. 55–85 MIN

    Decision mapping & handoff

    Trace who can inspect and change decisions. Prepare a design for another group to challenge.

02

Session two · 85 minutes

Challenge, repair
& a research agenda

  1. 00–30 MIN

    Cross-group challenge

    A routine changes, a service fails, a model changes a rule, or someone wants to leave. What breaks?

  2. 30–55 MIN

    Evaluation design

    Revise the design and specify evidence, plausible failures, evaluators, and remaining uncertainty.

  3. 55–75 MIN

    Collective synthesis

    Share methods and boundaries. Prioritize research questions while retaining disagreements.

  4. 75–85 MIN

    Permissions & close

    Review the outputs, attribution choices, contributor review window, and optional next steps.

A few practical questions

Before you join.

Has the workshop been accepted?+

Acceptance is pending. This website describes the proposed workshop. The session date, time, room, contribution route, and final deadlines will be confirmed after acceptance and organizer approval.

Do I need to code or bring a prototype?+

No. You can work with prepared sketches, paper, or editable text. No programming experience, working software, commercial AI account, personal device, or live internet connection is required.

Can I attend without a position paper?+

The planned workshop is open to all CHI attendees within capacity, with priority for accepted position-paper authors under CHI’s allocation rules. We expect 20–30 attendees including organizers; at least ten are required for a viable session. Conference registration and participation follow CHI’s confirmed rules.

Will I have to share personal health information?+

No. All exercises use fictional scenarios. Please do not submit personal health records or display private information during activities. You may pass on any activity. This workshop is not a study of attendees and does not provide clinical advice.

Will my paper or contribution be published?+

Only with your explicit permission. You may choose public posting, a link to your own copy, participant-only circulation, or no distribution beyond review. Artifact sharing and attribution are separate choices. No public permission is assumed, and no arXiv or CEUR-WS publication is promised.

How will access needs be supported?+

Instructions and materials will have accessible HTML and editable-text versions, with offline and paper options. You can speak, write, type, use facilitator assistance, or pass. Contact the lead organizer privately about specific access needs; conference arrangements such as live captioning and assistive listening remain subject to confirmation with CHI.
